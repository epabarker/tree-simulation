# M15: the cost of a frame, and moving work out of it

## Question
What does the page do on every frame of a tween, and how much of it can move out of the frame into once-a-season work? The target, from the M12 review, is a frame p95 of at most 12 ms on a mid-range phone. Desktop numbers stand in until the phone is measured; a phone is commonly 4–8× slower per core.

## Setup
```bash
node src/web/render/scene.bench.ts     # the Scene's per-frame CPU work, in Node (microsecond timer)
npm run build && npx vite preview      # then, in a browser:
#   /?bench&preset=coniferStand&seasons=80    the lab, at the panel's speed (3 seasons/s)
#   /garden.html?bench&seasons=40             the home-page view, through the intro
```
- **`scene.bench.ts`:** a scene tweening between seasons 79 and 80 of `forest` and `coniferStand` (seed 1, pseudo-depth on, broadleaf leaves). It times one frame (`computeGeometry(t)`, `colorIndices`, `leaves(5, t)`) and reports the median of 15 rounds of 40 frames after a warm-up.
- **Page `?bench`:** samples every animated frame's draw CPU time and frame interval, plus the worker's time per season. Firefox rounds `performance.now()` to a millisecond, so the page numbers are coarse.
- **Machine:** Intel i7-1260P, Node 24.18; headless Firefox via puppeteer-core at device pixel ratio 1.

## Result

**The Scene's CPU per tween frame** (Node):

| scene | metamers | leaves | before | after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| forest, season 80 | 1508 | 2418 | 0.39 ms | 0.07 ms |
| coniferStand, season 80 | 10135 | 13275 | 2.45 ms | 0.52 ms |

Before the change, the conifer stand's 2.45 ms split into:
- leaves: 1.78 ms (`atan2`, `cos`, `sin`, a hash and `hypot` for every leaf, every frame);
- geometry: 0.68 ms with pseudo-depth, of which 0.31 ms was each limb's projection, recomputed every frame;
- colour indices: 0.05 ms.

**What moved out of the frame:**
1. **Projections** are computed once per snapshot. Each limb's out-of-plane turn depends only on the season's final angles and ids.
2. **The leaf layout** is computed once per snapshot. That covers which leaves exist, each leaf's metamer and layer, its direction as a fixed rotation (a, b) of its shoot's direction, its size scale and its colour. A frame then normalises the shoot vector and applies the rotation.
3. **Colour indices** are cached per snapshot and colour mode.
4. **Geometry arrays** are pooled across snapshots.

**Equivalence:** I compared the old `Scene` with the new one on `forest`, `coniferStand` and `birch`, with depth on and off, at t = 0, 0.3, 0.77 and 1, at four seasons each: 96 checks. Leaf counts, back/front layers and colours are identical, and positions, directions and sizes differ by at most 6·10⁻⁸ (float32 rounding).

**WebGL2:**
- Each attribute buffer now grows to its CPU array's doubled capacity once, and frames overwrite it with `bufferSubData`. Before, `bufferData` reallocated every stream on every tween frame.
- The shade overlay is uploaded (or, in Canvas 2D, built) only while it is shown, once per snapshot.

**Page `?bench`** (draw CPU ms p50 / p95, rounded to whole ms by Firefox; frame interval p95):

| page | metamers | before | after | frame p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lab, `skyStand`, 60 seasons | 2340 | 1 / 2 | 1 / 1 | 17.3 ms |
| lab, `coniferStand`, 80 seasons | 10135 | 3 / 6 | 1 / 3 | 17.3 ms |
| lab, `forest`, 80 seasons | 1508 | 1 / 2 | 0 / 1 | 17.3 ms |
| `garden.html`, 40 seasons | ≈ 1600 | 1 / 2 | 0 / 1 | 17.3 ms |

Every case held 60 fps on the desktop before and after, so these numbers can't show jank; that has to be measured on a phone.

**Pipelining:** the worker now computes season s+1 while the page tweens to s. During the home page's intro (4 seasons a second), seasons arrive every 250–267 ms (3.8 a second). The worker takes 3–16 ms a season, and its time now hides behind the tween.

**Adaptive quality** (`render/quality.ts`):
- In each window of 60 animated frame intervals, a 90th percentile above 24 ms steps the pixel ratio down (2 → 1.5 → 1), then drops the back layer of leaves.
- Ten windows in a row under 18 ms step back up.
- Unit tests cover stepping down, settling after a change, stopping at the last level, stepping up, and resetting the streak. The desktop never triggers it.

## Conclusion
- The Scene's per-frame CPU work is about 4.7–5.6× smaller. A 10k-metamer stand needs ≈ 0.5 ms on this desktop, which leaves room for the hierarchical wind sway that M16 adds per frame.
- At home-page sizes (≈ 1.5k metamers) a frame's CPU work is now 0.07 ms in the Scene, plus filling and uploading the instance arrays.
- **On the user's phone** (garden-v0.2.0, `edbarker.dev/lab/?bench&preset=coniferStand&seasons=80`, 10,135 metamers): frame p95 16.8 ms, so it holds 60 fps through the tweens. The worker takes 18.15 ms a season on average (6.7 ms on the desktop) and runs alongside the tween, hidden by pipelining. The plan's target, "frame p95 ≤ 12 ms", was badly worded: the interval between frames cannot drop below 16.7 ms at 60 Hz. What it asked for, no dropped frames on a mid-range phone, is met. The draw time's own p95 was not in the reading.
- A GPU-side tween (the fallback the M12 plan kept for phones) is not needed unless the phone says otherwise.
