# M19: calibrating birch, oak, beech and fir

## Question
Can species patches (ADR 0016), fitted to published patterns, give each species its own shade tolerance, form and life history? Then, with no scripting, does a mixed forest of them go through birch → beech succession?

Expected beforehand:
- **Shade survival order:** fir ≈ beech < oak < birch.
- **Stand floors:** darkest under beech, lightest under birch.
- **Lone oak:** broad and squat.
- **Mixed forest:** birch takes the canopy early and loses it to beech. Oak hangs on only in gaps.

## Literature targets

| Pattern | Target | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Shade-tolerance score (0–5) | birch 2.03, oak 2.45, beech 4.56, silver fir 4.6 | Niinemets & Valladares 2006, Appendix A ([table](https://esapubs.org/archive/mono/M076/020/appendix-A.htm)) |
| Light a sapling survives at | tolerant ≈ 1–3%, intolerant 10–15% | Kobe et al. 1995; literature review §4 |
| Sapling growth vs light | beech outgrows oak at every light level; beech is optimal at 10%, oak needs > 20% | Ligot et al. 2013 ([link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/d383d761a8545573b277e77606038522/?utm_source=claude_desktop)) |
| Leaf economics | intolerant species: higher photosynthetic capacity and N per area; tolerant ones: long-lived, cheap leaves | Hallik et al. 2009 ([link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/9337178538b75b5680fa398374ae5c97/?utm_source=claude_desktop)) |
| Slenderness | stand-grown H/D 60–80; open-grown squat (≈ 40–55) | Watt et al. 2023; MacFarlane et al. 2017 |
| Open-grown crown | at maturity, crown width ≈ crown length | Hein et al. 2008 ([link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/c54923a1ccd658eb968b96c1c09d8e75/?utm_source=claude_desktop)) |

## Setup
```bash
uv run --project analysis python analysis/experiments/m19_species.py   # 200 runs, about 15 s
```

This is the final calibration after the realism pass of 2026-10-28, under M18's row-marched light.

**Growth rules.** All runs use the woodland's growth rules: the `forest` principles with R5's neighbour signal (M19 step 2). The calibration settings come from the `oakOpen` preset, so they have no O4 recruitment and no storms.

**Settings per species**, 5 seeds each:
- a lone tree, 160 × 160, 60 seasons;
- a stand of ten trees 21 cells apart, interior trees 2–7, 60 seasons;
- a shade house: a seedling under a roof one cell thick across a 40-cell periodic world, at eight roof densities, 30 seasons. The light under a uniform roof is computed exactly from its density, since the seedling's own crown shades the ground beneath it.

**Mixed forest:** the `succession` preset, a ring of 7 birch, 2 oak and 2 beech, 400 seasons, 5 seeds. Canopy means trees at least 30 cells tall.

**What the species differ by** (the knobs, all per tree):
- **Shade survival:** Q_ss (`r6.qMin`): birch 0.12, oak 0.07, beech 0.025, fir 0.02. Below it a resting bud is a leafless dormant bud, so a seedling whose one bud sits in deeper shade produces nothing and starves.
- **Leaf economics** (Hallik et al. 2009):
  - photosynthetic capacity α: birch 4, oak 3.5, beech and fir 3;
  - foliage cost m_f: birch 0.1, oak 0.08, beech 0.035, fir 0.05;
  - short-shoot leaf area a_ss: birch 1.6, oak 1.6, beech 2.6, fir 2.4. This sets how much shade each species casts.
- **Crown depth:** relative shedding T_rel (birch 0.4, oak 0.08, beech 0.03, fir 0.25). With R1's carbon shedding, T_rel is the only light threshold; the absolute T is unused. Birch also has a high release light (`r4.qRel` 0.3): with the default 0.1, light under birch woke epicormic shoots all down the trunk and the crowns never lifted.
- **Height:** the hydraulic scale L_h: birch 45, oak 75, the others 60.
- **Form:** R2 tables, plus:
  - **oak acrotony** κ = 3 (§R2.4, new): laterals at the top of each year's shoot outgrow those below, so the trunk divides into big limbs;
  - **oak mechanics:** stiffer wood and posture control;
  - **birch mechanics:** light leaves and less permanent sag, with limbs settling to 70° from vertical. The leader stays upright while the limbs spread.
- **O4 life history:**
  - seed cost: birch 2, oak and beech 10;
  - dispersal d: birch 40, oak 25, beech 12;
  - establishment light: birch 0.25, oak 0.1, beech 0.03;
  - background mortality: birch 0.008, the others 0.002.
- **Storms** (all species): windthrow now scales with slenderness, (H/D − 40)/30 clamped to [0, 2], so a squat open-grown tree stands. In 49 storms over 5 seeds, the lone oak was thrown 0 times; the forest lost a tree about every 4 storms.

## Result

| Species | Open H | Open W/H | Open H/D | Open branch angle | Stand H | Stand H/D | Stand crown ratio | Lowest light all seedlings survive |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Birch | 89 | 0.46 | 65 | 43° | 69 | 71 | 0.43 | 13% |
| Oak | 75 | 1.66 | 42 | 56° | 75 | 75 | 0.38 | 8% |
| Beech | 84 | 1.06 | 40 | 57° | 79 | 68 | 0.47 | 3% |
| Fir | 110 | 0.41 | 49 | 75° | 84 | 65 | 0.76 | 2% |

![shade house](figures/2026-10-27-m19-shade-house.png)

- **Shade tolerance.** The order and the bands match the literature: fir (2%) ≈ beech (3%) < oak (8%) < birch (13%). Tolerant species sit in 1–3%, the intolerant birch in 10–15%. At 27% light after 30 seasons, beech is about 22 cells tall, oak 12 and birch 21 (Ligot: beech outgrows oak at every light level). In full light birch is fastest.
- **Stand floors.** The floor darkens from birch (18–23% of the sky) to oak (5%) to beech (3%). Oak and beech seedlings can wait under birch, and the succession depends on that.
- **Early growth.** Birch leads the stand: 45 cells at season 15, against beech's 33.
- **Form.**
  - All stands are slender (H/D 65–75).
  - Open-grown oak and beech are squat (40–42) and broad: oak's W/H is 1.66, beech's 1.06.
  - The open oak's trunk divides into big limbs.
  - The lone birch is an open, narrow cone with branches down its stem (W/H 0.46).
- **Succession.** It emerges across seeds. Birch holds 4–5 canopy trees per run until season 100, then falls to 0.6 by season 400. Beech rises from 1.8 to 6.8. Oak holds about 1 tree, then dwindles, since it cannot regenerate under beech's 3% floor. This is the oak-to-beech replacement seen in old-growth beech–oak forests.

![succession](figures/2026-10-27-m19-species.png)

## Deviations and caveats
- **Birch and beech crown depth in pure stands are close** (0.43 and 0.47). The light-demander lifts its crown only slightly more. Beech's crown base is set by carbon: its own 3% shade starves its lower limbs.
- **Fir keeps a deep crown in a stand** (0.76 against roughly 0.5–0.7 measured). Fir is a lab species.
- **Windthrow ignores rooting and soil:** it depends only on height and slenderness. `oakOpen` still runs without storms, since breakage could spoil Plate II.
- **The shade house is a lab abstraction:** a uniform roof, with no sunflecks and no seasonal light under deciduous canopies.
- **The first calibration (2026-10-27, M6 light) is superseded.** It made birch a narrow pole and its seedlings too shade-tolerant (7%), and left the open oak excurrent. The devlog records the steps.

## Conclusion
- The four species differ only by per-tree patches and reproduce:
  - the published shade-tolerance order and bands;
  - Ligot's growth ranking;
  - realistic slenderness;
  - the darkening from birch to oak to beech;
  - an open-grown oak that breaks into limbs.
- Birch → beech succession, with oak dwindling, then emerges from local rules.
