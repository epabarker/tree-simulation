# M27: fitting all ten species by CMA-ES

## Question
Can CMA-ES find species patches that match literature traits, measured and described (ADR 0020), better than the hand-tuned ones? Do the fitted species still behave as they do in the wild, on what the fit never saw?

## Setup
```bash
uv run --project analysis python analysis/experiments/m27_fit.py --species all --skip-done --restarts 2,1
uv run --project analysis python analysis/experiments/m27_holdout.py
```

- **Targets:** `analysis/fit/targets/<species>.toml`; sources in the [research note](../research/2026-10-28-species-traits.md).
- **Discount:** every fit uses the hyperbolic hydraulic discount (ADR 0021).
- **Search:** CMA-ES over 17 parameters (18 from round 2, with h_j), population 12, 5 seeds a candidate, up to 100 generations a restart.
- **Final measurement:** the ten best candidates on 10 fixed seeds, beside the hand-tuned patch.

## Round 1 (2026-10-28)
- **Run time:** 1 h 51 min on the user's 12-core laptop.
  - Birch, beech, oak and fir: 3 warm and 2 random restarts.
  - The rest: 2 + 1. The first species ran on battery.
- **Results:** in `fits/round1/`; figures `figures/2026-10-28-m27-round1-*.png`.

**Fit** (loss on 10 fixed seeds):

| Species | Hand-tuned | Fitted | Traits missed by more than one band |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pine | 24.0 | 0.28 | none |
| Hornbeam | 22.7 | 0.87 | none |
| Maple | 5.3 | 0.88 | none |
| Willow | 45.0 | 1.05 | none |
| Rowan | 12.8 | 1.07 | none |
| Birch | 10.8 | 1.18 | none |
| Oak | 19.9 | 1.59 | open W/H 1.0 against 1.3 (half weight) |
| Holly | 2.3 | 2.43 | stand height 58 against an estimated 41 |
| Beech | 13.6 | 2.71 | crown fullness 0.65 against 0.75 (half weight) |
| Fir | 32.6 | 11.3 | H30/H60 0.57 against 0.42; stand height 104 against 80; floor 2.2% against 1.3% |

- **The search worked:**
  - **Restarts:** every species' best came from a warm restart. Random restarts reached similar losses.
  - **Generations:** many restarts were still improving after generation 50.
  - **Early stopping:** the 1% patience rule rarely fired, since lucky seeds count as gains.
- **Crown profiles:** birch's hourglass is gone, and beech's dome is wider at mid-crown.

**Hold-out** (`fits/round1/holdout.json`):
- **Shade tolerance drifted.**
  - Spearman ρ of survival light against Niinemets & Valladares's scores fell from −0.98 (hand-tuned) to −0.90.
  - 5 of 10 fitted species sat in their survival bands, against 8 of 10 hand-tuned.
  - Holly lost most of its tolerance: 8.5% against its 2–5% band, after the fit raised its Q_ss from 0.03 to 0.068.
  - Oak (5.4%) and beech (2.1%) became too tolerant.
  - Q_ss sat at its lower bound for beech, fir and hornbeam.
  - **Form alone does not pin shade tolerance.**
- **The mixed wood** (all eight fitted woodland patches at once, 400 seasons, 6 seeds):
  - succession held: birch led, then faded from 2.2 to 0.8 canopy trees a run, and beech rose to the most (3.7 at season 325);
  - the understorey thinned: willow was gone by season 200 and rowan by 300, holly and oak were down to 0.17 a run;
  - the hand-tuned wood kept all eight.
- **Lab woods:** fitted fir and pine reached 162 cells, the 160-cell world's top, by season 150.
  - Pine was 130 cells (32 m) at 100, against its table's 29 m.
  - The fit saw 60 seasons, so the hyperbolic tail went unchecked.
- **Allometry and release:** Δ 2.0 everywhere; the l–d and leaf–section slopes and the felled-gap release are much as hand-tuned.
- **Juvenile growth:** still too fast. Pine's α went to its lower bound (2.5), the fit's only way to slow it.

### Decisions after round 1 (the user, 2026-10-28)
- **Shade survival enters the fit** for the four species with measured sapling minimum light (Hagemeier & Leuschner 2019): birch 21%, oak 11.4%, beech 3.4% and hornbeam 3.3%. The other six's bands and the Spearman order stay held out (ADR 0020 addendum).
- **Late growth enters the fit:**
  - The stand trial runs on to season 100 for every species.
  - Its mean interior height at 100 over that at 60 (`stand.lateShare`) is a target: measured from the yield tables for oak, pine, beech, hornbeam, fir (half weight) and willow (half weight), estimated for the rest.
- **Fir's H30/H60** goes to half weight: its table is a mountain one, with 10–20 years to breast height.
- **The juvenile phase** (ADR 0022): shoots start short and lengthen as the tree grows to h_j, a new free parameter.
- **The round-1 fits are kept** in `fits/round1/`, and every species is refitted (round 2).
- **Cost:** a candidate now costs about 1.6× as much (smoke 42 s against 27 s).
