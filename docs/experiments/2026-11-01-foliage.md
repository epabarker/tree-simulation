## 1. Per-species bark (step 1)

One `--bark-young → --bark-old` ramp drew every species. Now each species' style names a bark (`render/bark-styles.ts`): three tokens in `scene.css` (`--<sp>-bark-twig`, `--<sp>-bark`, `--<sp>-bark-old`, light and dark), read by `theme.ts`. Age moves a stem from twig to bark, girth moves it from bark to old bark. Bark records the stem's girth, not the stand's age. WebGL2 adds a texture over the colour that also grows with girth: oak's deep fissures, maple's shallow ones, willow's diamonds, birch's dark marks and black base, pine's plates. Each pattern fades out below a few pixels' spacing, so it never shimmers. Canvas 2D draws the colour only.

| species | twig → bark → old | texture |
|---|---|---|
| birch | red-brown → white → black base | dark marks on the white; fissures and plates at the base |
| oak | olive-brown → grey-brown → dark grey-brown | deep fissures from early on |
| beech | olive-brown → pale grey → pale grey | none (smooth "into old age") |
| hornbeam | brown → steel grey | faint vertical streaks |
| maple | brown → grey-pale brown | shallow fissures |
| holly | green → silver-grey | none |
| rowan | brown → smooth grey | faint lenticels |
| willow | downy grey → grey-brown | diamond fissures |
| pine | grey-brown → orange-red (upper stem, limbs) → grey-brown plates (lower bole) | plates |
| fir | grey-brown → silvery grey | light plates when old |

**The girth thresholds are set on the model's girths.** Under ADR 0027's pipe radii, a closed stand's trunks at 100 measure 17–55 cm at the base: pine and birch about 20 cm, oak and beech 25–55. Lone trees at 60 measure 29–54 cm. That is about half a real stand's, so each threshold is about 0.55 of the real girth at which the bark changes. Pine's first try used real girths: its stand at 100 stayed orange to the ground.

Sheets: `figures/2026-11-01-foliage-bark-phone.jpg`, `figures/2026-11-01-foliage-bark-laptop.jpg`.

## 2. Clumping and oak's marcescence (step 2)

A style may carry a `clump` (`LeafClump` in `render/leaf-styles.ts`). It moves and turns the cards a young metamer already carries; it never adds or removes one (ADR 0024). `scene.test.ts` checks the count for every style. The light model never sees it.

| species | clump | the card's cue |
|---|---|---|
| oak | gather 0.95 over 4 metamers, fan ±1 rad, size ±35% | clots at the shoot tips |
| rowan | gather 0.95 over 3, fan ±1.3 | rosettes at the tips |
| pine | gather 0.85 over 4, fan ±0.9 | tufts at the limb ends |
| birch | spread back along the shoot, hang 0.55 | hanging strings |
| beech | spread back, level 0.65 | flat sprays |
| hornbeam | spread back, level 0.35 | level sprays |
| maple, willow, holly | spread back, small fan | even |
| fir | spread back | even |

**Oak's marcescence:** young oaks (under 20 cells, 5 m) keep their dead leaves through the winter, and so does the lower crown of an older oak (`marcescentBelow` 0.3 of its height, each card's limit varied from 0.5 to 1.5 of that so the held leaves thin out upward), after BSBI's "young trees, coppice and low epicormic branches". The lab's `?bench` takes `&tau=0.93` to hold the plate in winter for the sheet.

**What clumping cannot do:** the cards sit on the shoots the core grows. Birch's strings hang, but its twigs do not: pendulous twigs are track B's vocabulary. Oak's clots are as dense as its young shoots.

Sheets: `figures/2026-11-01-foliage-clump-phone.jpg` and `-laptop.jpg` (step 1 against step 2), `figures/2026-11-01-foliage-oak-winter.jpg`.

## 3. The coverage instrument and the two knobs (step 3, I3 step 4)

`render/coverage.ts` measures:
- the share of 1-cell picture cells holding a drawn leaf, per canopy half;
- the leaves per covered cell (the I3 plan's §1.2 measure, now taken on the leaves as drawn);
- the share of canopy pixels in leaf colour, read back from the canvas after a draw.

`?bench` reports it (`BenchResult.coverage`), and `garden.coverage()` measures on demand. Leaf opacity (0.92) and `LEAF_MIN_PX` (3.5) are now view options: the lab's View panel has `leafOpacity` and `leafMinPx`, and both pages take them as URL parameters. Nothing thins leaves.

`knobs.mjs`: the garden's phone plate (seed 10, the 40-season intro), redrawn at three settings of each knob.

Garden phone plate, seed 10, season 40, 12,353 leaves:

| setting | leaf pixels, canopy | upper half | cells covered, lower / upper | leaves per covered cell (upper) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| opacity 0.7 | 0.124 | 0.068 | 0.54 / 0.27 | 4.5 |
| **opacity 0.92, min 3.5 px (default)** | **0.135** | **0.072** | 0.54 / 0.27 | 4.5 |
| opacity 1 | 0.138 | 0.073 | 0.54 / 0.27 | 4.5 |
| min 0 px | 0.135 | 0.072 | 0.54 / 0.27 | 4.5 |
| min 6 px | 0.185 | 0.098 | 0.54 / 0.27 | 4.5 |

- **Opacity** hardly moves the leaf-pixel share: the test is a pixel's hue, and a 70% card over the sky is still green. What opacity changes is how much sky shows through each card, which the sheet shows and the share does not.
- **`LEAF_MIN_PX`** does nothing at 3.5 px or below on this plate: every card is already longer than that at the garden's scale. At 6 px the canopy's leaf pixels rise by 37%, the green wall the I3 plan warns of.
- The cell measures do not change with either knob, as they should not: they count the cards, not their pixels.

Sheet: `figures/2026-11-01-foliage-knobs.jpg`.

## 4. I3's remainder (step 4)

### Step 3: the snapshot's cost
`snapshot-profile.ts` measures the garden's planting (seed 10), WASM under Node, one lane, on a loaded VM. Each figure is the median of 5.

| plate | season | trees | metamers | season, ms | snapshot, ms | metrics alone, ms | metrics' share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 66 | 40 | 49 | 14.4k | 61 | 11.7 | 2.1 | 0.18 |
| 66 | 100 | 94 | 16.1k | 82 | 5.8 | 1.6 | 0.28 |
| 160 | 40 | 229 | 48.5k | 144 | 22.0 | 6.2 | 0.28 |
| 160 | 100 | 229 | 66.0k | 244 | 19.6 | 5.9 | 0.30 |

The per-tree metrics are 18–30% of the snapshot, and the snapshot is 6–22 ms against a 61–244 ms season. The plan's 17–148 ms was measured before recruits per area and `zOrder`. The plan's stop rule applies: the cost is not in the metrics, so no lean cadence is needed and the core is left alone.

### Step 5: reduced motion
`growstill.mjs`: `garden.html` with `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, seed 10, the time from navigation to `garden.ready`. In that time the worker grows the intro in one call (`growStill`) and draws the still plate.

These timings come from a VM under load (renders running beside them), three loads each:

| plate | metamers at 40 | to the still picture, median (range) |
|---|---|---|
| phone, 390 × 844 | 12.2k | 3.6 s (3.1–4.1) |
| laptop, 1440 × 900 | 31.3k | 5.5 s (5.4–13.1) |

The phone's wait is at the plan's threshold of about 3 s, and the laptop's is over it. By the plan's rule, the first-paint picture (`cli/first-paint.ts`, the 300 kB budget) is next if the user's phone confirms it. The phone's own figure is the user's to take: `garden.html` with reduced motion on.

### Step 6: Canvas 2D on the phone's plate
`bench.mjs`: the garden's `?bench` on the phone's plate, WebGL2 and Canvas 2D, software GL on this VM. These are relative numbers, not a phone's.

| renderer | metamers at 40 | draw p50 / p95, ms | frame p95, ms | long frames | worker mean / max, ms | leaf pixels |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| WebGL2 | 12.2k | 9.8 / 128.5 | 100 | 11 of 12 | 281 / 1603 | 0.134 |
| Canvas 2D | 12.2k | 18 / 465 | 100 | 102 of 178 | 97 / 300 | 0.102 |

- **Canvas 2D finishes the phone's intro.** It did not finish the lab's 60k-metamer mixed wood (I3 plan §1.4). On the phone's plate its draw p50 is about twice WebGL2's, and its p95 (the frame that takes a new snapshot) is 465 ms.
- It is the fallback and it works on this plate, with a hitch each season.
- Its leaves cover less of the canopy (0.102 against 0.134). It draws the outlines without WebGL2's half-pixel minimum width, so thin cards vanish.
- These numbers come from software GL on a busy VM, so the phone's own numbers are the ones to read.

### The 160-cell plate's memory (ADR 0026)
`memory.ts` runs one process per case, 200 seasons, with the garden's snapshot options:

| case | WASM memory |
|---|---|
| garden 160 | 211.4 MB |
| specimen (oakOpen, 160 wide) | 173.3 MB |
| garden 160 + specimen, both live (the worker until now) | **341.7 MB** |
| garden 160 + the specimen in an 80-cell world | 294.4 MB |
| **swap: the garden held as checkpoint bytes while the specimen grows** | **218.7 MB** (+ 20 MB of bytes outside linear memory) |

The specimen holds 173 MB by itself: an oak of 51k metamers that reaches the woods' top. Narrowing its world saves only the field, about 50 MB. The worker's `swap` now saves the forest, frees it, and grows the specimen in the space it left. Going back, it loads the forest from its bytes. The reloaded forest's hash is the one it was saved with, and `checkpoint.test.ts` already holds that a restored world grows on as it would have. So the peak is the larger world plus the checkpoint's buffer, not the two worlds' sum, and it is under the 256 MB budget.
