# The tracks: species identity, the form vocabulary, foliage, physiology (2026-11-01)

Written after round 5's form sweeps and the first night of its fit, with the user. It reframes the species work and sets up parallel sessions. Supersedes the sequencing in the [round-5 plan](2026-10-31-species-round5.md); that plan's harness and ADR 0029–0032 stand.

## 0. What changed in the reading

- **The question was asked backwards.** Four fit rounds tried to get species look out of physiology through nine form numbers. The sweep sheets, in one afternoon, showed what the fits could not: the ontogeny knobs set the kind of tree, posture and η do not, and the profile cannot see tiers. Knob-to-look, judged by eye, is the fast loop; it belongs before any fit.
- **The model's form vocabulary is too small.** R2 makes a spire, a tiered cone, a table or a vase. It cannot make a dome (limbs that ascend then arch), an oak's sinuous heavy scaffold, or a birch's pendulous shoots. No parameter search finds a shape the vocabulary lacks. Every system that produces species-specific trees (Weber & Penn 1995, Arbaro, SpeedTree, The Grove, Makowski et al. 2019) authors a species as a sheet of descriptive form parameters by eye, with physics layered on for behaviour. Our bet is that look emerges from physiology; the bet needs a vocabulary wide enough to express the looks.
- **Foliage is half of species identity at phone scale** (leaf size, colour, clumping, twig density) and iterates in seconds in the browser. The renderer already has a leaf style per species (M23); nothing in this effort has used it.
- **3D stays.** Its dividends are the stand: planting in depth, light competition in a volume, floors and leaf area now on measured values, H/D from a real pipe model, determinism with 20-seed evidence. The lone crown is where 3D has not paid off, and that is the vocabulary, not the dimension.
- **The slow loop is kept off the critical path.** A form question is minutes by eye; a stand's numbers are seconds by replay; physiology is hours and runs once per model change, overnight, never as the gate on a judgement.

## 2. Rules of the road

- **One branch per track** (`claude/research`, `claude/vocabulary`, `claude/foliage`, `claude/species-round5`), small PRs into `3d-port`, merged by the user. No track edits another's files; shared files (`params.ts`, `presets.ts`, `spec.md`) are B's, and B adds with defaults that change no golden. STATUS is E's; each track writes its lines in the devlog under its own heading.
- **Docs are the contract.** A track's deliverable is a file in the repo at a named path, with the command that regenerates any figure. A session reports to E when a deliverable is pushed, with the path and one paragraph; E reviews and digests for the user.
- **The user's time goes on pictures.** Atlas sheets, panels, the browser, the phone. Nothing numeric is put to the user that a session can settle itself.
- **Ask before diverging** from `spec.md` or an ADR (AGENTS.md); a new rule is an ADR proposal until the user accepts it on its sheet.
- **Fast loops first.** A question that can be answered by a sweep sheet or a browser reload is answered that way before any fit is proposed.

## 3. The week to shipping (revised 2026-11-01, the user)

**Done means what a visitor sees:** the mixed wood at 40–100 seasons reads as a wood of recognisable species, in depth, on the user's phone, within budget. The 20-seed evidence for `forest` (floor, H/D, crown base, plateau) is re-run once at the end under the R4 fix and ADR 0032; it is not a gate on the way.

**Moved to after shipping:** the 400-season hold-out woods (run and reported, not gated on); fir and pine; the lone-tree dome and further form rounds; measured shape targets (routes C and D); the C2 extinction measurement; C3–C6.

**Round 5 is the last fit before shipping.** A species that misses gets a hand override (ADR 0023's mechanism: an hour, judged on the panel), never a round 6. Birch's r0 bound is an override, not a re-fit.

**One judging session a day.** Everything that needs the user's eye is batched into one set of pictures.
