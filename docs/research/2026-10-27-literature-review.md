# Literature review: principles, 2.5D foliage, calibration (2026-10-27)

A second pass over the spec's principles (§3), using Consensus (Semantic Scholar/OpenAlex), alphaXiv and web search. It asks three questions: are the principles still sound, what is missing, and what does the literature say about the parameters still awaiting calibration (ROADMAP). It also looks at ways to make crowns look full without giving up 2D. The [decisions](#decisions-agreed-2026-10-27) were agreed on the same day and recorded in ADRs 0006–0008; the spec and ROADMAP were amended accordingly.

## Summary

1. **The core (C1–C6) still holds.** Newer work supports it or sharpens it. None of it contradicts the core.
2. **C5 should be relative, not absolute.** Recent experiments find that whether a branch dies depends on its light *relative to the rest of its own tree* (correlative inhibition), not only on its own carbon balance. This is the clearest gap in the spec.
3. **Short shoots are the biggest missing principle,** and they also answer the visual complaint. In species that have them, leaves on short shoots make up most of the leaf area, and they sit inside the crown.
4. **The "x-ray" look is the correct 2D result, but the picture is the wrong projection.** A real crown is also a hollow shell (Kitajima et al. 2004 measured 61% light extinction in the outer 0.5 m). What makes a real tree look full is seeing that shell *projected* onto a disc. A render-only depth layer gets most of the look and costs nothing in the simulation. A thick 3D slab is possible, but it is a different project.
5. **Calibration targets:** the current sky weighting (p = 1) is exactly a uniform 3D sky projected onto the plane. Trunks are about 4× too thick (H/D ≈ 14–19 against 60–80 in real stands), so r0 should be about 0.02. T and k_ext now have literature ranges keyed to shade tolerance.

## 2. What is missing

### 2.1 Short shoots (long/short shoot dimorphism)
Many trees build two kinds of shoot. *Long shoots* extend the crown. *Short shoots* (spurs) add a rosette of leaves each year with almost no extension. Millan et al. 2022 measured 30 woody species: where short shoots exist, they carry *most of the leaf area*, and their leaves cost 36% less biomass, so the tree can keep foliage in self-shaded parts of the crown ([link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/0e0df3ab7f505fe3b2522ce2e8844246/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). Yagi 2000, Kull et al. 2002 and Jarčuška et al. 2012 confirm the split for temperate trees, *Populus* and *Fagus* ([link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/98be3ec0027e52ba9049721fb8ce0ffa/?utm_source=claude_desktop), [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/857b34efceaf5dfb9e481c94b96e7549/?utm_source=claude_desktop), [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/e9f87173508c57b0bba7fe6128f2345b/?utm_source=claude_desktop)).

A possible local rule: a lateral bud whose v is below the C3 extension threshold (v < 1) but whose Q ≥ Q_ss becomes a short shoot. It adds no metamer, deposits a fraction f_ss of a unit of leaf area, contributes its Q to the basipetal sum, and is shed by C5 like any other branch. Cost O(buds). It would close loop A from inside the crown, raise production, and put leaves along older branches, where real trees have them. **This is not in the spec** (decision 1).

### 2.2 Correlative inhibition in shedding
C5 compares a branch's light per metamer with a fixed T. The evidence in §1 says the comparison is with the tree's best-lit branches. A minimal change: T_eff = max(T_abs, T_rel · Q̄_top), where Q̄_top is the tree's mean bud light in its upper crown, or its best-lit decile. With T_rel = 0, the rule is today's C5. This predicts that suppressed trees keep deeper crowns than dominant ones at the same local light, as Sprugel found. It may also ease the M6 "suppressed decurrent tree loses its whole crown in one season" issue. Decision 2.

### 2.3 Smaller gaps
- **A second cause of the height plateau:** reproductive allocation after maturity (Becker et al. 2000). R1 should treat it as an alternative to L_h, not as an extra.
- **Shade → upright branches** (Roychoudhry et al. 2017), folded into R5.
- **Rendered and simulated foliage differ.** C2 deposits one unit per *bud*, but the page draws leaves on every metamer up to 5 seasons old (`leafAge`). The leaves you see are not the leaves that cast shade. Either is defensible, but they should agree, or the difference should be stated (architecture.md).
- **Method: pattern-oriented calibration.** Fit several weak targets at once (H/D, crown base, crown width, bud-break fraction, Δ) rather than one strong one (Reynolds & Ford 2005; Wiegand et al. 2008, [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/2058a5d5e84053d3b6a2898507bb020a/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). Stava et al. 2014 (inverse procedural modelling) remains the reference for automating this.

## 3. A halfway house to 3D

### Why it looks like an x-ray
The thin foliage shell around a dark interior is what light competition produces, in 3D too (the spec's §C2.10 "hollow interior, dense shell"). Kitajima et al. 2004 measured 61% light extinction within 0.5 m of the surface of a late-successional crown. A real tree looks full for three reasons:

1. **Projection.** You see a 3D shell projected onto a disc: front and back foliage overlap the interior. The simulation shows a *cross-section* of the shell, which is a ring.
2. **Short shoots** hold leaves inside the crown and along old wood (§2.1).
3. **Laterals pointing at or away from the viewer** foreshorten and cover the trunk. In 2D every lateral lies in the picture plane.

### Options, cheapest first

| Option | What changes | Cost | Fidelity | Spec impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Render-only depth** | Each leafy metamer gets a pseudo-depth z = hash(birthId) and draws a small leaf cluster around its axis rather than one leaf. Back-layer leaves (z < 0) draw behind the wood, darker and desaturated (the far side of the shell); front-layer leaves draw over it. Laterals are foreshortened with the spec's own projected-spiral rule (§C1.4, β_eff = atan(tan β·\|sin φ_k\|)), and the core does not change. Optionally a faint back-fill of the crown interior from a dilated foliage mask | 2–4× leaf instances, instanced in WebGL2. No simulation cost | High visual gain. The light overlay no longer matches the leaves (keep a diagnostic "x-ray" view) | None: page only. Shared logic goes in `scene.ts` |
| **B. Short shoots** (§2.1) | Interior and older-wood foliage that is *simulated*: it shades and it is shed | O(buds) | Biologically grounded. Helps the look less than A, because the 2D interior is still dark | New principle (decision 1) |
| **C. Parallel transects** | Several independent 2D stands drawn one behind another with atmospheric perspective | Linear in the number of planes | Forest depth, not fuller single trees | None |
| **D. Thick slab (true 2.5D)** | Grid W × D × H with D ≈ 8–16, trees as 3D graphs (spiral phyllotaxis, direction vectors), sky sweep over K elevations × M azimuths, closed or reflecting depth walls | Light ≈ D·M × today (≈ 50× → ~150 ms/season for a stand); metamers ≈ 5–10×, which strains the phone renderer budget (28k metamers = 5.8 ms on desktop) | Real all-round crowns | Reverses spec §2 (Option P → a slab Option S). C1, C4, R3 become vector-valued. ADR needed. Effectively a new core |

**Recommendation:** A now, as a page-only change in a day or two, with a toggle between the natural and x-ray views. Then B as a principle, if you agree, because it is both botany and looks. Hold D unless you want trees that can be turned or seen at an angle. At that point it is better to prototype Palubicki's 3D model at small scale than to stretch the 2D core.

## 4. Parameters awaiting calibration

One finding underlies several rows. A headless run of `skyStand` and `skyTree` (50 seasons, seed defaults) gives tree heights of ≈ 95 cells, about 2 cells a season. If a cell is 0.2–0.3 m, a tree is 20–30 m tall at 50 years, which is plausible for a temperate stand. That gives the grid a physical scale.

| Param | Now | Literature anchor | Proposal |
|---|---|---|---|
| `c2.skyExponent` | 1 | **Derivation.** For a uniform 3D sky, horizontal irradiance arriving at in-plane angle ψ (the elevation seen in the vertical plane) is dE/dψ = L·(π/2)·sin ψ, which is exactly p = 1. For the standard overcast sky, L ∝ (1 + 2 sin h)/3, it is ∝ sin ψ·(π/2 + (8/3) sin ψ), which matches p ≈ 1.5 (1.47 at 15°, 1.54 at 30°) | Keep p = 1 (uniform sky) as the default. Offer p = 1.5 as an "overcast" preset |
| `c2.minElevation` | 15° | A uniform sky puts 1 − cos 15° = 3.4% of its weight below 15° on each side. In stands, neighbours block that light anyway | Keep |
| `c2.K` | 7 | No biological value. Spacing is 25°. Streaks are a sampling artefact | Convergence test K ∈ {7, 11, 15, 31} on bole, width and stalls. Or rotate the K directions by a golden-ratio offset each season (deterministic, no new RNG purpose) so streaks average out at the same cost |
| `c2.kExt` | 0.5 | Crown K = 0.35 (orthotropic) to 0.53–0.80 (plagiotropic), crown LAI 2–8 (Kitajima et al. 2004). Shade-tolerant seedlings survive at ≈ 1% of full light, intolerant ones need ≈ 10–15% (Kobe et al. 1995, [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/fdd89df93f0c51c6b7e94ebe4b6dc10b/?utm_source=claude_desktop); Petritan et al. 2007, [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/0ff75c1a304a519b8835686a69a8a17c/?utm_source=claude_desktop); Bloor & Grubb 2003, [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/71b78cd314615cc99d60b1d7f0a9d710/?utm_source=claude_desktop)) | k_ext has no direct physical value because leaf area per bud is arbitrary. Calibrate it on a *target*: mean ground light under the interior of a closed stand ≈ 2–10% of open sky. Then make it a species knob (≈ 1.5–2× between orthotropic and plagiotropic species, §9) |
| `c5.T` | 0.1 | Tolerant species prune at deeper shade (Kothari et al. 2025). Sapling survival thresholds of 1–2% (tolerant) to 10–15% (intolerant) | 0.1 is a mid-to-intolerant (birch/pine-like) value. Tolerant species ≈ 0.02–0.05. Then add T_rel (§2.2) |
| `c6.r0` | 0.08 | Slenderness H/D (both in metres) for stand-grown conifers is ≈ 62–80, risky above 80 (Watt et al. 2023, [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/52d3d52cd74957d499bcefe075bee6ab/?utm_source=claude_desktop)); "slender" above 100 (Stăncioiu et al. 2021, [link](https://consensus.app/papers/details/f3c07c675026582ea60650cd4b81a2f9/?utm_source=claude_desktop)). Open-grown trees are squat (MacFarlane et al. 2017). **Measured here:** stand H/D = 14–19, open-grown 10 | Radius scales linearly with r0 under the pipe rule, so r0 ≈ 0.02 gives stand H/D ≈ 56–75 and open-grown ≈ 41, both realistic. At 0.25 m/cell that is a 5 mm tip radius. The renderer will need a minimum on-screen width for twigs |
| `c4` (`wd, xi, eta, epsilon, sigma`) | 1, 0.3, 0.15, 0.5, 20° | Only qualitative (Palubicki Fig. 12). GSA biology says A_set should depend on order and light | Calibrate on patterns: first-order branch angle to vertical, crown width/height, and the M3 lean test. Wait for R2 to give A_set per physiological age |
| `c2.a, b, C` (triangle) | 0.15, 1.5, 1 | Legacy model, pinned to M1–M5 presets | Freeze. Not worth calibrating further |
