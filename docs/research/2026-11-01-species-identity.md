# Species identity: what makes each garden species recognisable at 15–30 m (track A, 2026-11-01)

Track A's first deliverable ([tracks plan](../plans/2026-11-01-tracks.md) §1, row A). One card per species, then the table **feature × species × where it lives today**, which tracks B (vocabulary) and C (foliage) act on. The cards serve the picture: a frontal 2D illustration on a phone, the tree 15–30 m away, so a cue counts if it survives at that distance.

## How the sources were read
The cloud container reaches only GitHub and a search engine. Wikipedia, Commons, PMC, the JRC atlas, Trees and Shrubs Online, Woodland Trust, Trees for Life, arboritecture.org, HAL and the publishers were all refused by the network policy. Every claim carries a tag:
- **[r]** read directly (the Arbaro presets and source, cloned from GitHub);
- **[s]** from the search engine's reading of the cited page: a close paraphrase, not a quotation. Check the page before relying on a number;
- **[u]** unsourced: field-guide knowledge from memory (Mitchell 1974; Johnson & More 2006, the Collins guide). Each is a claim to check, not evidence.

No limb angles, limb counts or open-grown W/H were found in any source for most species ([s] searches); the routes to them are Hasenauer 1997 and Pretzsch et al. 2015 (open-grown allometry), and QSMs (audit route C, [target audit](../reviews/2026-10-31-species-target-audit.md) §2). Opening forest.jrc.ec.europa.eu, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, arboritecture.org, treesandshrubsonline.org and commons.wikimedia.org in the environment's network settings would turn most [s] into [r].

Photographs are linked, never copied. The Commons category "Solitary *Genus species*" (under each species' category) is the best single place to look for open-grown silhouettes.

## Architectural models (Hallé, Oldeman & Tomlinson 1978)
| species | model | basis |
|---|---|---|
| beech | **Troll**: all axes plagiotropic at first; the trunk is built by secondary straightening of their bases; sympodial | Pfisterer 2000 ("Fagus type") [s]; Nicolini 2000, Thiébaut 1982 [s]; Millet et al. 1998 (F. grandifolia) [s] |
| oak | **Rauh**: rhythmic, monopodial, orthotropic axes, lateral flowering | algorithmicbotany.org `catm.tree2000` lists oak, ash, pine, maple, larch as temperate Rauh [s]; Pfisterer 2000 [s] |
| birch | **not assigned in any source found.** Millet et al. 1999: *B. populifolia* "related to Rauh's model … although sympodial"; birch keeps one hierarchic system for life (no reiterated levels) | [s] |
| hornbeam | **not found**; Troll by analogy with beech (distichous, plagiotropic shoots) | [u] |
| goat willow | **not found**; Salix long shoots are sympodial (subterminal bud) | Flora of North America, *Salix* [s] |
| rowan | Rauh, probably ("Quercus type" in the Pannonian table) | Architekturmodelle des pannonischen Raumes [s] |
| field maple | **Rauh**, "Acer type" | Pannonian table [s]; Pfisterer's "Acer–Pinus type" [s] |
| holly | **Massart** (regular tiers on an orthotropic trunk), the hierarchy declining in old trees | Pfisterer 2000 [s] |
| silver fir | **Massart** | Pfisterer 2000 [s] |
| Scots pine | **Rauh** | Barthélémy, Édelin & Hallé 1989 [s] |

Pseudoterminal buds (the shoot tip aborts, so every long shoot is sympodial) are listed for *Betula*, *Carpinus* and *Salix* (Forestry Chronicle 2008 [s]); oak has a true terminal bud in a cluster of subapical buds (bugwood keys [s]). The model is about the young tree; every species here except birch, fir and young pine leaves its model through reiteration by maturity (Millet et al. 1999 [s]; Barthélémy & Caraglio 2007, not readable here).

## Silver birch (*Betula pendula*)
**Silhouette.** Young: narrowly conical; older: oval to irregular, "open and airy" (NCSU; EUFORGEN [s]). RHS: narrowly conical, to 25 m, with slender drooping twigs [s]. 15–25 m, exceptionally 30 (JRC [s]). The crown is see-through at 20 m; the white trunk shows through it [u]. Stand: a tall clean bole and a small high crown; live crown ratio of measured trees 0.60 (0.29–0.91) (Forests 12:599 [s]).

**Scaffold.** One straight trunk that persists to the top, unless damaged young (first-nature; BSBI Fermanagh [s]): excurrent and hierarchic for life (Millet 1999 [s]). Main limbs: many, slender, of similar thickness, rising at ≈ 40–50° from vertical near the trunk and **arching outward along their length**, their outer thirds level or falling [u]; BSBI: "fine long-pendulous shoots hang from elegantly arched branches on a straight trunk" [s]. No heavy scaffold: the thickness hierarchy is trunk ≫ limbs ≈ one another [u].

**Twig habit.** Branch tips and young twigs hang (Kew [s]): long, thin, unbranched hanging shoots in curtains, the cue that separates *pendula* from the erect *pubescens* (Trees and Shrubs Online [s]: habit alone is not decisive between the two). Short shoots carry the leaves and female catkins; long shoots end in male catkins; short shoots can revert to long (Ishihara & Kikuzawa 2004 [s]). Leaf-off: a fine purplish-brown haze around the limbs [u].

**Foliage at distance.** Leaves small, 2.5–7.5 cm, triangular, doubly toothed (NCSU [s]); light mid-green; first to flush, golden yellow early in autumn (earthwatch [s]). Clumped in pairs on short shoots along the hanging twigs, so the foliage hangs in strings and the crown reads as a fine, light, dappled mass [u]. Not marcescent [u].

**Bark and trunk.** Silvery white with black diamond fissures; reddish-brown when young; the base turns dark and rugged with age (Trees for Life [s]). At 20 m the white trunk and limbs are the strongest single cue [u]. Often a slight lean toward light [u].

**Ontogeny.** 20 y: a conical young tree with a leader. 40–60: the weeping twigs develop, the crown becomes oval and open. 60–100: the end of its life (usually 60–90, sometimes 150; Trees for Life [s]); crown thins, limbs die back [u]. A tree whose crown was suppressed young never recovers (Forests 2021 [s]).

Arbaro has no birch; its `quaking_aspen` (Salicaceae, a light pioneer) is the nearest sheet [r]: Shape 7 (tend flame), level-1 DownAngle 60 / V −50, Curve −40. The Grove has a "Silver Birch" quick-start preset and a paper-birch twig [s].
Photos: [winter](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Betula_penuda_at_sunset_in_winter.jpg); [frost, Finland](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Betula_pendula_-_Riippakoivu_DSC00010_C.JPG); [open-grown](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Betula_pendula_tree_Bulgaria.jpg); [category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Betula_pendula).

## Oak (*Quercus robur*, *Q. petraea*)
**Silhouette.** Open-grown robur: "spreading and unevenly domed", massive low limbs, broad and gnarly (Trees and Shrubs Online; Bot. Soc. Scotland [s]); often wider than tall [u]. Petraea: narrower, more upright and straighter-branched, the trunk running further into the crown, more regular (SAPS; Woodland Trust [s]). Stand: tall, narrower, a long bole (IOS journal [s]). The outline is **lumpy**: separate clumps of foliage on separate limbs, with sky between them [u].

**Scaffold.** JRC atlas: robur's main stem "tends to disappear in the crown, developing irregular boughs with twisting branches"; petraea "usually develops a main stem with boughs gradually decreasing in size" (Eaton et al. 2016 [s]). So robur is decurrent: a short bole (3–6 m [u]) dividing into 3–6 massive limbs [u]. The limbs are **sinuous and elbowed**: when a terminal bud is lost, a subapical bud takes over and leaves a kink (Sternberg, IOS journal [s]); lammas shoots add more (bugwood [s]). Lower limbs go out near level, even fall and turn up at their tips; upper limbs ascend at 30–60° [u]. A steep thickness hierarchy: a few heavy limbs, then abruptly fine twigs [u].

**Twig habit.** Stout twigs with buds clustered at the tip (treeguideuk [s]); short annual shoots, many forks, so twigs are dense and **clustered at the ends** of the limbs; zig-zag at a fine scale [u]. Leaf-off the crown reads as a dark, angular, twisted scaffold carrying dense tufts [u].

**Foliage at distance.** Leaves ≈ 10 cm, deeply lobed (Woodland Trust [s]); yellow-green at the late flush, then dark green; russet late in autumn [s/u]; marcescent on young trees and lower, inner branches (IOS [s]). Leaves crowd at shoot tips around the bud cluster: a clotted, dark, coarse texture [u].

**Bark and trunk.** Deeply fissured, grey-brown [u]; epicormic shoots on the trunk after thinning or stress, "cat's-claw" bud pads and burrs on old trees (Bartlett [s]). Trunk thick for its height; open-grown H/D low [u].

**Ontogeny.** 20 y: an oval crown with a leader [u]. 40–80: the leader is lost, the crown broadens into the dome (JRC [s], [u]). 100+: massive limbs, wider; very old trees "stag-headed", the crown retrenching (oakleafgardening [s]).

Arbaro's `ca_black_oak` (*Q. kelloggii*) [r]: Shape 2 (hemispherical); trunk splits (0BaseSplits 2, 0SegSplits 0.4: decurrent); level-1 limbs long (Length 0.8) and steep (DownAngle 30, V −30), **Curve 40 then CurveBack −70** (bend out, then back up) with CurveV 150 (a strong random kink); level 2: 120 branches, short (0.2).
Photos: [winter hedgerow oak](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Quercus_robur_in_hedge.jpg); [open-grown](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Oak_tree_-_Qu%C3%A9rcus_r%C3%B3bur.jpg); [category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Quercus_robur).

## Goat willow (*Salix caprea*)
**Silhouette.** "Large, spreading, rounded shrub or small tree", 8–12 m (RHS [s]); ≈ 10 m (Woodland Trust [s]). Untidy, broad, often leaning [u]. Stand: an edge and clearing tree; shade-intolerant (USGS [s]); it leans out of the wood to the light [u].

**Scaffold.** A short thick trunk, branching from the base; an obovoid crown of "many ascending branches … and more divergent twigs" (illinoiswildflowers; asturnatura [s]). Often several stems from a low fork [u]. Sympodial long shoots (FNA [s]). Limbs straight to slightly curved, ascending at ≈ 30–45° and splaying [u].

**Twig habit.** Thick, stocky winter twigs, greenish-yellow to reddish-brown (UCC [s]); ascending, not pendulous [u]. Moderate density [u].

**Foliage at distance.** Broad, oval to round leaves 5–12 × 2.5–8 cm, dull grey-green above, felted grey-white below (TCV; Wildlife Trusts [s]): **grey-green and silvery in wind** [u]. Silver then yellow catkins in March, before the leaves (MBG [s]): the spring cue. Dull yellow in autumn [u].

**Bark and trunk.** Grey-brown, smooth, then diamond fissures (asturnatura [s]).

**Ontogeny.** A short-lived pioneer (USGS [s]; sources conflict on lifespan: 15–30 y, illinoiswildflowers, against "can live 300", Woodland Trust [s]). At 20–40 y a broad bush-tree; it then falls apart, its limbs layering or sprouting [u].

Arbaro has only `weeping_willow` (*S. babylonica*), not this habit [r].
Photos: [mature, spring](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:(ms)_Salix_caprea_15.jpg); [habit](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Salix_caprea.jpg).

## Beech (*Fagus sylvatica*)
**Silhouette.** 30–40 m, to 50 (JRC atlas, Houston Durrant et al. 2016 [s]). Young: conical, narrow; old: broad, very densely branched, ovoid to round (asturnatura [s]). Open-grown: a **huge dome**, its low branches sweeping down to the ground (treeguideuk [s]). Stand: a tall smooth column and a high, dense, narrow crown; the "cathedral" wood [u]; the crown's width/height is constant with height (J. For. Sci. 2017 [s]).

**Scaffold.** Troll's model: young or weak trees have a leaning, plagiotropic main axis, bilateral symmetry and a flat crown; vigorous adults a vertical axis and a wide crown (Nicolini 2000 [s]); the stem alternates plagiotropic and orthotropic phases and straightens secondarily throughout life (Thiébaut 1982 [s]). The mature tree carries **main branches near the top of the stem that form a terminal fork**, each a "total plagiotropic reiterate" repeating the young tree (Nicolini, [s]). Open-grown: the trunk divides at 3–8 m into several steep, smooth limbs of similar size that **rise steeply and then fan out and arch over**, building the dome [u]. Forking is more frequent in deep shade and in the open (Nicolini & Caraglio 1994 [s]).

**Twig habit.** Slender, grey, zig-zag twigs (Woodland Trust "twigged" [s]); long, pointed copper buds standing out at > 45° (Nature's Calendar [s]). Shoots in **flat horizontal sprays**: shaded branches form a Horn monolayer of large, level, evenly spaced leaves (Packham et al. 2012, Biological Flora [s]). From a distance the crown is layered in near-level plates [u]. Leaf-off: a very fine, dense tracery [u].

**Foliage at distance.** Leaves oval, 5–10 cm, wavy-edged (Woodland Trust [s]); bright lime green with silky hairs at flush, then dark green; yellow to copper and fox-red in autumn (Forestry England; Country Life [s]). Marcescent on young trees and hedges (Woodland Trust [s]): a copper winter cue. Dense, opaque crown [u].

**Bark and trunk.** Smooth, grey (Woodland Trust [s]): pale, smooth, often elephant-skinned limbs, the second-strongest trunk cue after birch [u].

**Ontogeny.** 20 y: a slender, layered cone; plagiotropic and leaning in shade (Nicolini [s]). 40–60: the crown broadens, limbs straighten up. 100+: the dome on a terminal fork of reiterates (Nicolini [s]); life 150–300 y (JRC [s]).

The Grove has a European beech twig [s]; Arbaro has none.
Photos: [solitary, Het Loo](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Fagus_sylvatica.JPG); [category, incl. "in winter"](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Fagus_sylvatica).

## Hornbeam (*Carpinus betulus*)
**Silhouette.** Conical young, then ovoid to rounded (van den Berk [s]); 20–25 m (JRC [s]). Dense, oval-rounded, smaller and finer than beech's dome [u].

**Scaffold.** A crown of "many close weak forks" (Trees and Shrubs Online [s]). Young branches grow steeply up; in old age they bend level, hang and break (storianaturale; van den Berk [s]). Lower branches near level, upper ones ascending (UAEX [s]). The trunk divides low into several steep, competing stems, so the crown is a sheaf of ascending limbs [u, consistent with "many close forks"]. Coppiced and pollarded (Woodland Trust [s]).

**Twig habit.** Fine, dense bare branching (COD [s]); buds pressed to the twig, which separates it from beech (glosnats [s]). Distichous sprays like beech's, less flat [u].

**Foliage at distance.** Leaves oval, pointed, 7–12 cm, doubly toothed and pleated (woodlands.co.uk [s]); yellow to orange in autumn [s]; marcescent, especially when clipped (EUFORGEN [s]). Fine and dense [u].

**Bark and trunk.** Smooth steel-grey, **fluted and "muscled"**, the ribs stronger with age; twisting (Trees and Shrubs Online; Morton Arboretum; Wildlife Trusts [s]).

**Ontogeny.** 20 y: conical [s]. 50–100: rounded and dense on several stems [u]. Old: limbs droop and break [s]; lives 250–350 y (Woodland Trust ATI [s]).

The Grove has a European hornbeam twig [s].
Photos: [habit, Karlsruhe](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Carpinus_betulus_001.JPG); [Hunsrück](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Carpinus_betulus_-_Hunsr%C3%BCck_001.jpg); [old tree](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Carpinus_betulus_(G%C3%BCrgen).JPG).

## Rowan (*Sorbus aucuparia*)
**Silhouette.** Slender, to 15–20 m, a narrow crown (Raspé et al. 2000, Biological Flora [s]); upright oval young, open at maturity (Oregon State [s]); "loose, roundish or irregular but wide", often multi-stemmed (van den Berk [s]); fruit loads bend the branches until the crown is almost round (ebben [s]). Light, open, airy [u].

**Scaffold.** Monopodial, branches erect to spreading at acute angles (Raspé [s]). A few (3–6) steep, straight limbs at ≈ 30–40° from vertical that splay outward with age and fruit [u].

**Twig habit.** Stout grey to reddish twigs with large purple, grey-hairy buds (Nature's Calendar; TCV [s]); sparse and coarse; spur shoots carrying leaf rosettes and flower heads [u].

**Foliage at distance.** Pinnate leaves 12–25 cm, 6–9 pairs of leaflets (Trees and Shrubs Online [s]), held in **rosettes at the shoot tips**: a feathery, see-through texture [u]. Creamy flat flower heads in May–June, scarlet berry bunches from August; orange to red autumn colour [u].

**Bark and trunk.** Smooth, shiny grey with lenticels (leafyplace [s]).

**Ontogeny.** Seeds from ≈ 15 y (BSBI [s]); short-lived: rarely beyond 80 y (several [s]). Narrow young, broader and rounder old [s]. In stands an understorey and edge tree (agroatlas [s]).

Photos: [category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Sorbus_aucuparia); bioimages [58670](https://bioimages.org.uk/image.php?id=58670).

## Holly (*Ilex aquifolium*)
**Silhouette.** Young trees straight and narrowly conical; old ones can round (waldwissen [s]); a "pyramidal tree up to 25 m" (Kew POWO [s]), usually 6–15 m [s]. A dark, dense cone, often skirted to the ground; broader and irregular in age [u]. Shade-tolerant understorey under oak and beech; long-lived, > 250 y (Peterken & Lloyd 1967, via ACCS [s]).

**Scaffold.** Massart: tiers on a persistent leader, the hierarchy declining in old trees (Pfisterer 2000 [s]). Many slender branches, level to slightly ascending, short [u]; often multi-stemmed [u].

**Twig habit.** Green, dense, alternate [u].

**Foliage at distance.** Leaves 5–10 cm, very glossy, dark green [u]; spiny low, spineless and flat high up (heterophylly, waldwissen [s]). Glossy specular highlights on a near-black mass; red berries on female trees in winter (dioecious, ACCS [s]). Evergreen: the same picture leaf-on and leaf-off.

**Bark.** Smooth, pale silver-grey [u].

**Ontogeny.** Narrow cone young, rounder old [s].

## Field maple (*Acer campestre*)
**Silhouette.** "To 25 m, spreading, domed crown" (Collins key, via treezilla [s]); ≈ 12 m tall and 7–8 m wide (van den Berk [s]): W/H ≈ 0.6 for a nursery tree. A compact, dense, rounded dome on a short trunk (RHS; tree-guide [s]); asymmetric where branches grow irregularly (ebben [s]).

**Scaffold.** Rauh, Acer type (Pannonian table [s]). Branches grow diagonally upward and branch richly (tree-guide [s]); trunks often curved and several (ebben [s]). Opposite branching; decurrent: a low division into several ascending limbs [u].

**Twig habit.** Opposite; greenish to reddish-brown twigs with corky ridges (Trees and Shrubs Online [s]), strongest on hedge plants (ebben [s]); dense, fine [u].

**Foliage at distance.** Small, 3–5-lobed leaves, 6–10 cm (van den Berk [s]); matt mid-green; **butter-yellow** in autumn, sometimes orange (Trees and Shrubs Online; BSBI [s]). A fine, dense texture [u].

**Bark.** Grey to pale brown, shallowly fissured with age (Trees and Shrubs Online [s]).

**Ontogeny.** A hedgerow shrub under cutting; open-grown, a low broad dome (Collins [s]).

The Grove sells a field-maple twig [s]. tree-gen's `acer` (genus only) [s]: Shape 4, base splits and segment splits, so a decurrent split-trunk scaffold.
Photos: [category](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Acer_campestre).

## Silver fir (*Abies alba*), briefly
Conical young, cylindrical later; old trees flatten into a "stork's nest" top (JRC atlas; MDPI Forests 15:998 [s]), sometimes dividing into several ascending stems (Trees and Shrubs Online [s]). Massart: an annual whorl of level branches with upturned tips [u]. Needles 2–3 cm, glossy dark green with two white bands below, reading silvery (NCSU [s]). Bark silver-grey, smooth with resin blisters [s]. tree-gen's `balsam_fir` (source not stated) [s]: Shape 0, level 1 DownAngle 50 / V −45 (lower branches lower), Curve −40.

## Scots pine (*Pinus sylvestris*), briefly
Conical young; it then loses its lower branches and builds a broad, flat-topped, irregular crown (COD; illinoiswildflowers [s]): a long bare trunk topped by a rounded or flat mass (conifers.org [s]). Open-grown trees are broad and gnarled; old "granny pines" stand alone in heather (naturblick; NatureScot [s]). Rauh [s]; lateral growth is concentrated in the top 40% of the crown (Ilonen et al. 1979, Silva Fennica [s]). Old limbs few, heavy and sinuous, level then upturned, carrying flat plates of foliage clumped at their ends [u]. Needles paired, twisted, blue-green, 3–7 cm, kept 2–4 y (FLS; COD [s]). **Bark orange-red and flaking on the upper stem and limbs, plated grey-brown below** (conifers.org [s]): the strongest cue at distance. Photo: [habitus](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:PinusSylvestris.jpg).

### What the table says, in order of what matters at 20 m
1. **Limb curvature along the limb** is in four of the eight garden species' first lines (birch's arch, beech's fan-and-arch, oak's elbows, hornbeam's bow), and is missing. Weber–Penn's `nCurve` and `nCurveBack` express it as two angles per level [r]; track B's first rule.
2. **Pendulous twigs that do not take the limbs with them** (birch, beech's sweeping lower limbs): missing; `levels.2.setPoint` ≥ 80 collapses the crown instead.
3. **The trunk's division into several co-dominant limbs** (oak, beech, hornbeam, maple, willow): missing as a rule; `pTerm` drops the leader but does not make *n* equal leaders. Weber–Penn's `0BaseSplits` and `nSegSplits` [r], or a sympodial reiteration (track B).
4. **Per-species bark** (birch white, pine orange, beech pale grey): a renderer change, likely the cheapest large gain at distance.
5. **Foliage clumping** (oak's clots, rowan's rosettes, pine's tufts, birch's strings) and gloss (holly): track C.
6. **Sinuous, elbowed axes** (oak, old pine): a subapical takeover at a lost tip, not random noise.
7. Fruit and catkins (rowan, holly, willow): seasonal colour, low priority.
